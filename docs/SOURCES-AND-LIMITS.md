# Sources, provenance, and limits

## Primary conceptual source

The protocol formalizes Nikita Beregovykh's sketch and discussion on 30 October 2026: a fixed central actor chamber, a changing agreement area, one representative per side, one full correction orbit returning to the logical beginning, and persistent coexistence. The later additions specify neutral terms, finite-time reasoning, mistake acknowledgement and forgiveness, and professional SI-assisted evaluation through a mutually approved independent agency without procedural veto powers.

The photograph is not republished in this repository. Its circular ideas are transcribed and clarified in the mathematical model. The named politicians and real conflict labels in the sketch are not treated as verified representative mandates.

## External reference actually consulted

**United Nations, United Nations Guidance for Effective Mediation (June 2012).**

- [Official landing page](https://peacemaker.un.org/en/documents/united-nations-guidance-effective-mediation)
- [Official PDF](https://peacemaker.un.org/sites/default/files/document/files/2022/10/guidanceeffectivemediationundpa2012english0.pdf)

The official source was retrieved during the initial discussion on 30 October 2026. Its mediation principles include consent, impartiality, inclusivity, ownership, legal frameworks, coordination, and the quality of agreements. Its treatment of inclusion distinguishes participation in the process from a seat in the main negotiation and cautions against assuming conflict parties represent the wider public. These principles informed the proposed representation and stakeholder provisions.

The UN document does not endorse this protocol, its circular geometry, the single-round rule, its definition of no veto, or any SI system. Those are original design proposals here.

## Verification status for this release

Attempts to refresh external web sources while preparing this repository returned unavailable/error results. Consequently this version claims no newly verified current conflict facts, complete global data access, current legal determination, or independent professional endorsement. The earlier retrieved UN guidance is used only for the general principles identified above.

The requirement for current data is a deployment requirement specified in [Expert evaluation](EXPERT-EVALUATION.md), not something fulfilled by the fictional example. Conflict-specific legal, technical, humanitarian, security, and cultural conclusions must be evaluated by qualified professionals under an actually approved mandate.

## Limits and research questions

- Equal seats do not equalize military, economic, informational, or coercive power.
- A circle does not prove fairness; a summary area depends on its chosen coordinates and measure.
- One round bounds opportunity and time. It does not guarantee agreement or dynamical stability.
- An agreed agency is not automatically independent; appointment, funding, incentives, and challenge routes need evidence.
- No-veto procedure cannot coexist with a guarantee that every actor will voluntarily authorize the same mandate or outcome.
- Neutral wording cannot make incompatible obligations compatible or resolve facts by euphemism.
- Mathematical error is narrower than factual uncertainty, political disagreement, harm, or responsibility.
- SI/AI professional reliability must be demonstrated for its actual use. This repository supplies no such certification.
- The record validator checks structure and selected cross-field invariants only. It does not verify signatures, credentials, observations, interpretation, fairness, or peaceful conduct.

Priority research: test order effects, representative capture, data poisoning, strategic delay, false consensus, evaluator dependence, the fidelity of neutral normalization, and the cost of closing before important new evidence can be considered. Compare the single-round design with other bounded procedures using realistic, independently reviewed simulations.
