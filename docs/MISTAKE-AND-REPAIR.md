# Mistake acknowledgement, forgiveness, and repair

The protocol treats fallibility as a normal feature of reasoning under finite time and incomplete information. It makes correction visible and preserves dignity. It does not treat every conflict as a mathematical misunderstanding.

## Classification

| Category | Example | Appropriate response |
|---|---|---|
| Arithmetic or unit error | Adding quantities expressed in incompatible units | Recalculate with units and trace affected conclusions |
| Formal inference error | Claiming a conclusion follows when an explicit counterexample satisfies the premises | Exhibit the inference and repair or withdraw it |
| Factual error | Relying on a demonstrably outdated measurement | Correct the fact and reevaluate dependent claims |
| Translation or interpretation error | Rendering a conditional offer as an unconditional obligation | Preserve original text and correct the interpretation |
| Model limit | A prediction fails under an unmodeled condition | Record the limit and revise confidence or applicability |
| Value disagreement | Different priorities or desired futures | Preserve disagreement and negotiate conduct boundaries |
| Contested legal or factual claim | Evidence or authoritative interpretation remains disputed | Use the specified professional review and retain uncertainty |

Values, culture, identity, reluctance to cooperate, and refusal to forgive are not mathematical errors. Harm, coercion, or deliberate deception must not be relabeled as an innocent calculation mistake without evidence.

## Error signal

A material error record should contain:

1. `error_id`, responsible actor, time, and the precise earlier claim/version.
2. Category and status: `alleged`, `acknowledged`, `confirmed_by_review`, or `disputed`.
3. What was wrong and the reproducible evidence or reasoning that shows it.
4. Corrected statement, residual uncertainty, and dependent conclusions affected.
5. Time limits, missing information, or misconceptions that contributed, without presenting them as automatic excuses.
6. Immediate procedural effect: amended analysis, invalidated evaluation, withdrawal, unresolved closure, or subsequent review.
7. A repair proposal and an optional, culturally appropriate forgiveness request.
8. The recipient's response, including no response or refusal, without penalty.

The duty to correct a known material error applies equally to representatives, agency staff, and evaluators. An allegation alone does not authorize the agency to speak a confession in someone else's name.

## Suggested neutral acknowledgement

> I identified a mistake in [claim or calculation]. The corrected statement is [correction], based on [evidence]. This changes [affected conclusion]. My earlier reasoning was limited by [specific condition], and I remain responsible for correcting its effects. I propose [repair]. Where appropriate, I ask forgiveness for the consequences of this mistake; your response is yours to decide.

The parties may use their own cultural form while preserving the factual correction. An apology is not a precondition for access to evidence or participation. Forgiveness does not delete the original entry, transfer responsibility to the recipient, waive rights, or establish that the repair is complete.

## Timing

During the single correction opportunity, a self-correction can change the final proposal. After that opportunity, factual corrections remain recordable, but they cannot create a disguised second proposal round. A material defect invalidates the affected candidate; the session closes unresolved or unsafe if an eligible previously reviewed alternative cannot be ratified in time.

After closure, append the correction and apply only previously authorized monitoring or incident procedures. A newly proposed obligation requires a new negotiation and new consent.
