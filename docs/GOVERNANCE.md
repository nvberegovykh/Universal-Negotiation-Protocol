# Governance, independence, and no veto

## Agency authorization

Every side approves the same agency mandate and conflict guidelines before the cycle begins. Approval identifies the institution, named responsible officers, funding, relevant expertise, conflicts of interest, methodology, and replacement process. There is no claim that an agency satisfying these requirements currently exists for any particular conflict.

Independence means demonstrable separation of professional judgment from parties' instructions, financial pressure, and undisclosed interests. The mandate should disclose funders and material relationships, separate facilitation from evaluation and appeals, provide balanced access to expertise, and define an independent complaint reviewer. A party's approval alone cannot prove independence.

If no common agency is authorized by the setup deadline, the session is `not_started`. Another mutually chosen agency or a different process can be considered outside this cycle.

## Exactly one representative

One seat means one authorized channel for the side's decisions. It does not mean one person has unlimited authority over a population. The record must specify the source, scope, duration, and ratification limits of the mandate.

Affected communities need structured input and access to relevant impact findings. The roster must address contested representation, dependency between purported sides, and controlled proxies. Splitting a side into multiple names does not automatically create additional seats. Conversely, genuinely distinct affected groups must not be erased merely to preserve a convenient two-player model.

A substitute inherits the same seat and correction status. Equal access includes interpretation, disability accommodations, relevant evidence, and adequate preparation; resources and timing must be established before entry.

## Exact meaning of no veto

| Action | Rule |
|---|---|
| Reject an agency before entry | Possible; the common mandate has not formed |
| Suppress another side's admissible evidence or proposal | Prohibited |
| Erase an unfavorable evaluation or dissent | Prohibited |
| Stop the clock, grant extra corrections, or change thresholds alone | Prohibited |
| Prevent a scheduled closure record because its outcome is disliked | Prohibited |
| Impose a settlement through an agency ruling or AI score | Prohibited |
| Decline one's own new obligation | Preserved; record unresolved where acceptance is necessary |
| Seek review of an eligibility decision | Available under the locked challenge procedure |
| Trigger a pre-agreed emergency closure with supporting evidence | Available under an objective, reviewable rule; not an unlimited veto |

The agency also has no discretionary veto: it may apply the agreed eligibility rules, with reasons and a review route, but cannot substitute its preferences for those rules. No actor can invalidate a properly completed procedure merely because it dislikes the result.

There is a genuine design tension: requiring approval from every side gives each side the ability to prevent this particular common authorization from forming. Unanimous voluntary authorization and a rule forbidding all refusal cannot both be guaranteed. This protocol locates unanimous approval at entry and removes special procedural veto powers after entry. It preserves consent to substantive obligations throughout.

## Deadlock and limited agreements

A refusal does not erase the other side's offer or prevent closure. The record identifies what is accepted, what remains disputed, and which conditions would need to change. No response is not consent.

A partial agreement requires assessment of dependencies and all affected interests. A subset may proceed only with the required explicit consents and without imposing new obligations on non-participants. Labeling an agreement “partial” is not a way to bypass someone whose rights or commitments it changes.

Neutral fallback terms must be independently lawful and authorized in advance. The protocol does not assume the current distribution of power or resources is fair, nor does it authorize a new distribution by default.

## Misconduct, replacement, and records

Misconduct allegations use a pre-agreed, evidence-based review route. An independent reviewer may establish disqualification under stated criteria; a preauthorized replacement procedure transfers the role without restarting the correction round. If no valid replacement exists before the deadline, close unresolved or unsafe.

Records should be append-only, access-controlled, and exportable to every side according to the confidentiality agreement. A digest helps detect changed content; it does not establish authenticity. Production records need verified identities, actual signature verification, reliable clocks, retention rules, and appropriate protection for sensitive testimony.

The protocol has no autonomous enforcement authority. Implementation, monitoring, and remedies require explicit lawful mandates beyond the document itself.
