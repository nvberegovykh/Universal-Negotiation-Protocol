# Fictional example: two communities sharing a pump

Everything here is synthetic. No real engineering, legal, peacekeeping, or SI evaluation has taken place.

Two communities, East and West, retain separate councils, cultural practices, and accounts of an earlier scheduling dispute. They share a pump maintenance obligation. The session concerns access to maintenance records and how planned servicing is notified; it does not settle ownership, water rights, or the historical dispute.

## Entry

Each community appoints one authorized representative. They approve a fictional independent agency, a common glossary, one correction opportunity each, and a hard closure time. Relevant residents have an impact-submission channel. The agency has no power to impose a resource allocation.

The starting record contains evaluated initial proposals and a bounded clarification log. Both sides acknowledge that the previous schedule was reported in inconsistent time zones. They retain their distinct interpretations of why the dispute happened.

## One correction round

Both representatives submit their final corrections against the same starting record through sealed submissions. The common release exposes compatible terms: shared read access to the maintenance log, the same notice interval for both communities, and an independent incident-review channel. Neither side gets a second correction because it dislikes the other's explanation.

The final combined package is separately evaluated. The fictional example marks its required conditions as satisfied to demonstrate the record format. A real deployment would require actual professional reviewers, verified data, and genuine signatures.

## Stable neutral closure

After final review, the package's exact text remains unchanged for the agreed observation window. No unresolved doubt is material to these narrow terms. Different interpretations of the earlier dispute remain recorded and outside the accepted package.

Each side explicitly ratifies the same final text. The procedure returns to its initial checkpoint with the corrections, evaluations, and acknowledgements preserved. Their separate identities and institutions remain. Monitoring continues only for the accepted obligations.

## Error acknowledgement

East's representative acknowledges converting a time-zone offset incorrectly, identifies the affected claim, and offers a correction and an apology. West acknowledges the correction but does not grant forgiveness during the session. The apology does not become a condition of agreement and West receives no penalty for its response.

## Alternate endings

| Event | Protocol outcome |
|---|---|
| A side attempts a second correction | Reject that extra correction; preserve the attempt in the audit record |
| A final source expires before closure | Refresh/review within the deadline or close unresolved |
| A representative does not respond | Dispose of the opportunity at its deadline; never infer consent |
| The final calculation contains an unresolved material error | No stable adoption; close unresolved or unsafe |
| One party declines the common package | Preserve the offer and refusal; close without imposing new obligations |
| A new danger appears after closure | Apply the previously authorized incident procedure; new terms need new consent |
